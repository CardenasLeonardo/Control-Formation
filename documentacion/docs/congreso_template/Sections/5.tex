\section{Conclusion}

A distributed control scheme for formation navigation of differential-drive multi-agent systems was presented, integrating an error-based consensus protocol with the Feasible Velocities Polygon for inter-agent collision avoidance. Unlike existing approaches, the proposed scheme explicitly preserves the non-holonomic kinematic constraints of each agent without resorting to linearization or model simplification. The anisotropic representation of neighboring agents through the FVP, constructed from LiDAR measurements, constitutes a fundamentally different alternative to isotropic potential fields, capturing the directional nature of the differential-drive robot in its interaction with the environment. Three simulation scenarios of increasing complexity were validated in Gazebo under a ROS2 framework: average consensus with collision avoidance, leader-following, and V-formation maintenance with dynamic offset rotation. In all cases, the agents converged toward the desired formation objectives while preserving collision-free trajectories.

Future work will extend the scheme to environments with static and dynamic obstacles, explore richer consensus protocols, and investigate the incorporation of a final orientation constraint into the control law, which would influence agent trajectories and enable orientation-aware consensus.
